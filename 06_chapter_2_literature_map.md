## 1. Core Chapter 2 argument

The literature review should build one central argument:

Thai IT students cannot directly observe the true future value of an IT micro-credential, so they judge it through visible signals such as formal recognition, stackability, and industry endorsement. Those signals shape trust, and trust shapes perceived value.

## 3. Key papers for Chapter 2

## 3.1 Foundation and policy papers

### Spence (1973)

- Use for the main logic of `Signaling Theory`.
- Core idea: when quality is not directly observable, people rely on signals.

### Becker (1964)

- Use for `Human Capital Theory`.
- Core idea: learners invest in education when they believe it improves future outcomes.

### Suchman (1995)

- Use for legitimacy.
- Core idea: things gain acceptance when they are seen as appropriate, credible, and institutionally supported.

### OECD (2021)

- Source: *Quality and value of micro-credentials in higher education: Preparing for the future*
- Use in Chapter 1 and Chapter 2 for the broader higher education context.
- Core use: quality, transparency, and value concerns are central to micro-credentials.
- Link: [OECD 2021](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/quality-and-value-of-micro-credentials-in-higher-education_9c4ad26d-en.html)

### OECD (2025)

- Source: *OECD Skills Strategy Thailand*
- Use for the Thailand policy context.
- Core use: Thai MOOC, lifelong learning, skills policy, and the National Credit Bank System.
- Link: [OECD Skills Strategy Thailand](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-skills-strategy-thailand_153a1fe6-en.html)

### UNESCO Bangkok (2025)

- Use for current Asia-Pacific recognition and lifelong learning discussions.

## 5. Strongest writing move for Chapter 2

Do not write Chapter 2 as:

- "micro-credentials are useful"
- "students may like them"
- "technology is changing"

Write it as:

- there is a live policy and higher education problem
- micro-credentials are promising but not automatically trusted
- the literature shows value depends on design, recognition, and legitimacy
- student-side causal evidence on these features is still limited

That final point is your gap.
