# Professor Thesis Blueprint

This blueprint is based on the reference files in this folder:

- `ITM7000 Individual PROJECT 1#.pptx` to `ITM7000 Individual PROJECT 6#.pptx`
- `671_9024_Thanpisit Mayurachayakul_Report.pdf`
- `TAM_Microcredentials_Thailand_Research_Proposal kimi.docx`

It reflects a quantitative, theory-based thesis structure centered on:

- a clear dependent variable (DV)
- at least three independent variables (IVs)
- optionally one mediator
- a conceptual framework derived from established theories
- measurement items adapted from prior studies
- survey-based data collection
- hypothesis testing with reliability and regression/SEM analysis

## 1. What the professor appears to expect

### Topic design

- Narrow, practical, and measurable topic
- Clear target population
- Clear location/context
- At least one established theory or model, often TAM, UTAUT, TPB, SOR, or TRA
- At least three IVs and one DV
- A mediator is preferred when theoretically justified

### Proposal expectations

Your proposal should usually include:

- Title
- Introduction
- Statement of problem
- Research objectives
- Theoretical framework
- Methodology
- Contribution
- Keywords

## 2. Recommended chapter structure

### Chapter 1: Introduction

1.1 Background of Study
- Start broad, then narrow to Asia/Thailand/local context if relevant
- Use current trends, statistics, and industry or education context
- Explain why the topic matters now

1.2 Statement of Problem
- State the practical or academic problem
- Identify the gap between current knowledge and what needs to be known
- Explain why your study is necessary

1.3 Research Objectives
- One objective per main relationship you will test
- Keep them measurable and aligned with hypotheses

1.4 Research Questions
- Convert objectives into research questions

1.5 Scope of Research
- Location
- Target population
- Variables
- Method

1.6 Research Limitations
- Sampling limitations
- Self-report bias
- Geographic limits
- Time constraints

1.7 Significance of the Study
- Theoretical significance
- Practical significance

1.8 Definition of Key Terms
- Define each major construct clearly
- Usually one final working definition per term

### Chapter 2: Literature Review

2.1 Applied Theory or Theories
- Introduce each theory
- Explain core constructs
- Explain why the theory fits your study

2.2 Variable Definitions
- Define each variable using prior literature
- Move from broader definitions to the one you adopt for your study
- The slides suggest multiple citations per variable

2.3 Relationship Between Variables
- Review evidence for each hypothesized relationship
- Highlight support, contradictions, and gaps

2.4 Previous Studies
- Summarize the most relevant empirical studies in table form
- Include author, topic, variables, method, and findings

### Chapter 3: Research Framework

3.1 Theoretical Framework
- Present the borrowed theories or models
- Explain how they support your variable relationships

3.2 Conceptual Framework
- Show a diagram of IVs, mediator if any, and DV
- All arrows should be justified by theory or prior research

3.3 Research Hypotheses
- Write one hypothesis per relationship
- Example format: `H1: Perceived usefulness has a positive effect on behavioral intention.`

3.4 Operationalization Table
- Variable
- Definition
- Measurement items
- Scale
- Source/reference

### Chapter 4: Research Methodology

4.1 Research Design
- Quantitative
- Usually cross-sectional survey
- Explanatory/descriptive as appropriate

4.2 Sampling Procedure
- Target population
- Sampling method
- Sample size rationale
- Inclusion and exclusion criteria

4.3 Research Instrument / Questionnaire Design
- Screening questions
- Demographics
- Scale items for each construct
- Source of items from prior studies
- Likert scale

4.4 Data Collection
- Where data comes from
- How the survey is distributed
- Timeframe
- Ethics, consent, and confidentiality

4.5 Statistical Treatment of Data
- Data cleaning
- Reliability testing
- Descriptive statistics
- Mean and standard deviation
- Regression or PLS-SEM
- Mediation analysis if applicable

### Chapter 5: Research Results

5.1 Reliability Testing
- Cronbach's alpha for each variable

5.2 Descriptive Analysis
- Demographic profile

5.3 Mean and Standard Deviation for Study Variables
- Mean and SD for each construct

5.4 Hypothesis Testing Results
- Regression coefficients or path coefficients
- p-values
- R-squared
- VIF if applicable

5.5 Additional Analyses
- Mediation analysis if used

### Chapter 6: Discussion and Recommendation

6.1 Discussion of Findings
- Interpret each result
- Compare with prior studies
- Explain unexpected findings

6.2 Theoretical Implications
- Explain contribution to theory

6.3 Practical Implications
- Give recommendations for relevant stakeholders

6.4 Limitations of the Study

6.5 Suggestions for Future Research

6.6 Conclusion

### Research objectives

- To examine the effect of [IV1] on [DV].
- To examine the effect of [IV2] on [DV].
- To examine the effect of [IV3] on [DV].
- To examine the effect of [Mediator or other variable] on [DV].
- To examine whether [Mediator] mediates the relationship between [IV] and [DV].

### Research questions

- What is the effect of [IV1] on [DV] among [target population] in [location]?
- What is the effect of [IV2] on [DV] among [target population] in [location]?
- What is the effect of [IV3] on [DV] among [target population] in [location]?
- Does [Mediator] mediate the relationship between [IV] and [DV]?

### Hypotheses

- H1: [IV1] has a positive effect on [DV].
- H2: [IV2] has a positive effect on [DV].
- H3: [IV3] has a positive effect on [DV].
- H4: [Mediator] has a positive effect on [DV].
- H5: [IV] has a positive effect on [Mediator].
