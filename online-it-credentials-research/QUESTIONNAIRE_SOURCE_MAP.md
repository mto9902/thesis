# Questionnaire Source Map

## Recommended primary instrument

Use the four core constructs as measured in Hunsinger and Smith (2008), article pp. 252-254. The article prints the wording, response anchors, scoring logic, pilot procedure, and reliability evidence.

| Construct | Published measurement | Number of responses | Published evidence |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Behavioral Intention | Three intention stems completed with the target behavior; seven-point bipolar agreement response | 3 | Hunsinger & Smith (2008), p. 252; alpha = .94 |
| Attitude | Three semantic evaluations of pursuing the target behavior: good/bad, positive/negative, and helpful/unhelpful | 3 | Hunsinger & Smith (2008), p. 253; alpha = .92 |
| Subjective Norm | Six normative-belief ratings paired with six motivation-to-comply ratings for IT managers, professors, hiring managers, advisors, parents, and the general public | 12 | Hunsinger & Smith (2008), p. 253; test-retest r = .56 |
| Perceived Behavioral Control | Four control-belief ratings paired with four facilitation ratings for learning ability, knowledge, skills, and money/resources | 8 | Hunsinger & Smith (2008), pp. 253-254; test-retest r = .54 |
| **Total** |  | **26** |  |

Hunsinger and Smith explain that alpha is not appropriate for the paired belief composites because the separate beliefs are not assumed to be internally consistent. The pilot and analysis plan should therefore follow the source's scoring logic instead of promising alpha for every construct.

## One required contextual substitution

The source wording refers to "IT certification." The proposed thesis concerns a different but related credential type. Every occurrence should therefore be changed consistently to:

**a short online IT professional certificate**

The 12-month time frame should remain unchanged. The thesis should say:

> Questionnaire items were adopted from Hunsinger and Smith (2008). The target phrase "IT certification" was replaced consistently with "short online IT professional certificate" to match the defined study context; the constructs, item meanings, referent groups, response anchors, and time horizon were otherwise retained.

This is an adaptation, not verbatim copying. Calling it verbatim would create the exact source-loophole the project is trying to avoid.

## Secondary online-learning questionnaire evidence

Wang (2023) prints a 28-item five-point questionnaire in its appendix, pp. 44-46. It covers Perceived Usefulness, Perceived Ease of Use, Attitude, Subjective Norm, Perceived Behavioral Control, and Behavioral Intention toward MOOCs.

It is useful for three purposes:

- showing that the same TPB constructs have been measured among undergraduates in an online-learning context;
- checking whether the pilot participants understand online-learning language; and
- providing a documented alternative if Professor Kimi specifically prefers a uniform five-point scale.

It should not silently replace the primary instrument. Its Behavioral Intention questions concern future MOOC use, recommendation, and sharing experience, which is not the same outcome as pursuing a certificate.

## Pilot requirement

The source study piloted its questionnaire with 27 students before the main survey. A pilot is also necessary here because the target phrase and national context change. The pilot does not create new questions. It checks whether Thai IT students interpret the adopted questions and the credential definition as intended.
