# Source Audit

## Audit rules

A source is suitable for the central study only when it provides most of the following:

1. A published framework diagram or an unambiguous tested model
2. Explicit hypotheses for the proposed relationships
3. Empirical testing of those relationships
4. Full questionnaire wording in the article or an accessible appendix
5. A behavior close to pursuing an online IT professional certificate
6. A student population reasonably close to Thai IT students

The labels below distinguish direct evidence from papers that are only useful for background.

## Core sources

| Source | Published framework and hypotheses | Questionnaire evidence | Fit and decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hunsinger & Smith (2006), *Predicting the Intention of Undergraduate IS Students to Earn IT Certification* | Figure 2 prints the complete student model with Affect, Cognition, Attitude, Subjective Norm, Perceived Behavioral Control, and Behavioral Intention. H1-H5 are printed. | Describes the planned measures but does not print the final item wording. | **Core framework source.** It studies the correct population and credential-pursuit decision. Use the figure as an original published framework, not as a generated reconstruction. |
| Hunsinger & Smith (2008), *Factors that Influence Information Systems Undergraduates to Pursue IT Certification* | Prints H1-H5 and tests them with 120 US information-systems undergraduates. Attitude, Subjective Norm, and Perceived Behavioral Control all significantly predict intention. | Article pp. 252-254 print the complete wording and scoring procedure for all constructs. The instrument was piloted with 27 students. | **Core empirical and questionnaire source.** It is the closest source found. Its credentials were primarily traditional vendor certifications, so the new target phrase must be disclosed as a contextual substitution. |
| Wang (2023), *Perception and Behavioral Intention Toward MOOCs: Undergraduates in China* | Figure 1 and H1-H5 integrate TAM and TPB. The paper independently supports Attitude, Subjective Norm, and Perceived Behavioral Control -> Behavioral Intention with 525 undergraduates. | The appendix on pp. 44-46 prints all 28 items on a five-point agreement scale. | **Corroborating framework source.** It confirms the same three core paths in online learning. Its intention items concern using, recommending, and sharing MOOCs rather than earning a certificate, so it is not the primary questionnaire. |

## Context and comparison sources

| Source | What it genuinely supplies | Why it is not the central source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Miao et al. (2024), *Intention to Use Technology for Micro-Credential Programs* | A published TAM and self-determination model tested with 474 university students in Karachi; direct micro-credential context. | It measures technology use for micro-credential programmes, not the decision to earn an external certificate. The article reports item sources and loadings but does not print the full questionnaire wording. |
| Mohan, Upadhyaya, & Pillai (2020), *Intention and Barriers to Use MOOCs* | An extended UTAUT2 model, 412 Indian postgraduate students, and a full questionnaire appendix. | The dependent items measure continuing to use MOOCs. They do not measure first-time pursuit of a professional certificate. |
| Singh (2022), *Examining Higher Education Students' Intention of Adopting MOOCs* | A TAM-based model, 33 printed items, and a sample including management and IT students in Mumbai; Coursera was the most used platform. | The behavior is MOOC-system adoption, frequent future use, and recommendation, not certificate pursuit. |
| Gao et al. (2025), *A Preliminary Study on Key Variables Related to Students' Continuance Intention to Pursue Academic Micro-Credentials* | Full wording for 51 UTAUT/ECT items and reliability results from 31 Malaysian students. | It is a preliminary reliability study of students who had already used a university academic micro-credential. It does not test the proposed paths and measures continuance. |
| Kuo (2025), professional certificates among beauty practitioners | A published professional-certificate framework and tested TPB-related relationships. | Different profession and non-student population; the article does not print the exact questionnaire statements. |
| Ilias et al. (2022), internal-auditing certification among Malaysian accounting students | A TPB model, hypotheses for pursuing professional certification, and item wording in Table 5. | Its instrument was adapted from an earlier accounting study and does not map as closely to short online IT certificates as the Hunsinger and Smith instrument. |
| Navanitha et al. (2022), digital-badge motivation instrument | Instrument-development and pilot evidence in a computing micro-credential context. | The article does not print all final 64 items; the later thesis containing them is restricted to registered users. |
| Abu-Dawood (2025), social motivation for Coursera micro-credentials | A Google/Coursera professional-certificate context and ten printed social-motivation statements. | Conference study of students already assigned or enrolled in a course; descriptive social-motivation analysis rather than a tested intention model. |
| Coursera learner and micro-credential reports | Current examples, platform terminology, learner motivations, and market context. | Provider-commissioned reports are not substitutes for peer-reviewed theory, hypotheses, or a validated independent questionnaire. |

## Rejected shortcuts

- Do not describe Miao et al. (2024) as full questionnaire evidence. The item wording is not printed.
- Do not describe Gao et al. (2025) as a validated causal framework. It reports reliability and descriptive results from a 31-person preliminary study.
- Do not treat MOOC continuance intention as the same outcome as initial intention to pursue a certificate.
- Do not treat Coursera as the issuer of every credential. It is often the delivery platform.
- Do not claim that wording is verbatim if "IT certification" has been changed to "short online IT professional certificate."

## Overall conclusion

The defensible route is a contextual replication of the three core TPB paths tested by Hunsinger and Smith, independently supported in the online-learning setting by Wang. This gives the thesis an actual published framework, directly tested hypotheses, established variables, and a fully printed source questionnaire without adding speculative constructs.
