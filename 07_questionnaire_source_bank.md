# Questionnaire Source Bank

This file helps answer your professor's requirement that the study should be supported by prior research and use a questionnaire that can be adopted or adapted.

## 1. Important position

Your current study uses a `survey experiment`, not a standard one-block opinion survey.

That means the questionnaire has two parts:

- `experimental manipulations`
  - these are designed by you from theory and prior literature
- `measured scales`
  - these should be adapted from prior studies wherever possible

So the questionnaire does **not** need every item to come from one single prior paper. It is academically normal to:

- adapt signal wording from policy and design literature
- adapt trust items from credibility or trust literature
- adapt value items from perceived value and employability-benefit literature
- pilot the final wording

## 2. Best source logic for your questionnaire

## 2.1 Experimental manipulations

These are not taken as ready-made scales. They are vignette features built from the literature.

## 2.2 Student trust scale

Recommended positioning:

- `adapted scale`
- built from trustworthiness and credibility literature
- contextualized to the micro-credential setting

Recommended source base:

- Suchman (1995)
- Ohanian (1990)
- Marttunen and colleagues (2021) on credibility evaluation of online sources

Recommended use:

- use simple Likert wording around legitimacy, credibility, reliability, and confidence
- do not use celebrity or advertising wording directly
- adapt the underlying credibility logic only

Current draft items already follow this approach:

- TR1: I would trust this micro-credential as a legitimate learning qualification.
- TR2: This micro-credential appears credible to me.
- TR3: I feel confident that this micro-credential would represent real learning.
- TR4: I would regard this micro-credential as reliable evidence of skill development.
- TR5: This micro-credential appears worthy of serious consideration.

These are defensible as:

- adapted from source-credibility and legitimacy literature
- finalized through pilot testing in the Thai student context

## 2.3 Perceived value scale

Recommended positioning:

- adapted from perceived value literature in higher education and learner contexts

Recommended source base:

- Ledden, Kalafatis, and Samouel (2011), *The measurement of perceived value in higher education: a unidimensional approach*
- Iskandar, Wirawan, and Salam (2024), *Students' perceived value in higher education institutions: investigating the role of antecedents and context*
- Ha, Van Dyke, and Spittle (2025), especially for perceived benefits and employability-oriented micro-credential value

Current draft items already fit this logic:

- PV1: This micro-credential would be valuable for my academic or career development.
- PV2: This micro-credential would be worth the time required to complete it.
- PV3: This micro-credential would improve my learning or employability prospects.
- PV4: This micro-credential would be a useful addition to my qualifications.
- PV5: Overall, this micro-credential appears worthwhile.
- PV6: This micro-credential would provide meaningful benefits relative to the effort involved.

These are defensible as:

- adapted from higher education perceived-value literature
- sharpened for the micro-credential context
- strengthened by recent employability-focused micro-credential studies

## 2.4 Enrollment intention scale

Recommended positioning:

- exploratory only
- not the conceptual center of the thesis

Support with:

- Ha, Van Dyke, and Spittle (2025), for student interest in micro-credential offerings
- your experimental design, where intention is secondary to value

Current items:

- EI1: I would consider enrolling in this micro-credential if it were available to me.
- EI2: I would be interested in learning more about this micro-credential.
- EI3: I would be likely to pursue a similar micro-credential in the future.

Say:

That is a normal and defensible answer.
